02 — Frequently Asked Questions

Straight answers,
before you tender.

常見問題

The questions consultants, main contractors and developers ask us most often about scope, standards, approvals and programming in Hong Kong.

What does an E&M contractor in Hong Kong do?

An E&M (electrical and mechanical) contractor installs, tests and commissions the electrical, mechanical and containment systems inside a building — including cable trunking, cable tray, floor trunking, wiring and earthing. In Hong Kong, E&M contractors usually work as specialist subcontractors to main contractors or directly for developers, and hold the relevant EMSD registrations for the electrical work they carry out.

How long does a cable trunking material submission take?

A complete submission package is typically approved in about five working days when the specification clauses, datasheets, test certificates and finish samples are issued together. Our first-pass approval rate is around 92%; the remainder usually involves finish or fire-rating comments that require a resubmission.

What is the difference between cable trunking and cable tray?

Trunking is an enclosed section with a removable lid, used where cables need mechanical protection and a neat appearance — ceiling voids, risers and exposed wall runs. Cable tray is an open, perforated or ladder-type support used where cables are fixed and accessible, such as plant rooms and data halls. Many projects use both on the same route.

Which standards apply to cable trunking in Hong Kong?

Most specifications reference BS EN 50085 for trunking and BS EN 61537 for cable tray and ladder, with BS EN 10346 covering the zinc coating on galvanised steel. Fire-rated containment is supported by tested systems and certificates, and earthing continuity is verified to BS 7430. Consultants also reference the local Code of Practice for the Electricity (Wiring) Regulations.

How much spare capacity should a trunking run have?

Sizing comes from the cable schedule, not a rule of thumb. Most Hong Kong specifications cap cable fill at roughly 40–45% of the internal cross-section and require spare capacity for future circuits, with the exact limit taken from the consultant’s specification and the manufacturer’s capacity table. For in-screed duct we normally keep at least one third of the usable section free and maintain a physical divider between power and data, so a later fit-out can add circuits without breaking the floor.

Can floor trunking be installed in an existing screed?

In-screed duct requires the screed to be cast around it, so retrofit usually means cutting and re-screeding along the route — disruptive and rarely cost-effective. For occupied buildings we normally recommend perimeter or surface trunking, raised floor outlets, or a planned cut-and-reinstate package carried out in phases.

Do you work as a subcontractor to main contractors?

Yes. Most of our work is specialist containment and electrical packages let by main contractors, with the balance direct to developers and facility owners. We provide technical submissions, shop drawings, method statements, programme input and O&M documentation as part of the package.

Which sectors do you cover in Hong Kong?

Commercial offices, healthcare, hospitality, residential, data centres and transport infrastructure. Each sector has different submission routes and working-hour constraints — live hospitals and operating railway stations, for example, are delivered in agreed engineering hours with night-shift supervision.
